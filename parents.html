For parents and guardians

A creative learning day, with a clear purpose.

Build Day helps students understand how digital games are designed and made. The goal is not more screen time for its own sake — it is a guided opportunity to plan, create, test, and explain an idea.

What your child practices

They are learning how to make choices, not just how to move parts around.

Students work on a small, achievable game concept. Along the way, they practice creative problem-solving, sequencing, communication, and responding to feedback — the same habits that show up in many kinds of learning.

  • Plan a goal and explain what a player is supposed to do.
  • Build a simple environment in Roblox Studio using visual tools.
  • Test an idea, notice what is confusing, and make a revision.
  • Reflect on what they made instead of comparing who “won.”
A fair definition of success

Students will not all finish identical projects. A meaningful outcome is a small playable prototype plus a clear explanation of what they learned and what they would try next.

No leaderboard required.
Build Day can be run around progress, teamwork, and reflection rather than scores or public rankings.

Safety and supervision

A school event should feel organized, supported, and safe.

When Build Day happens during the school day, the classroom teacher or school facilitator remains the main point of supervision. Families do not need to manage the event from home.

  • 01
    The school uses its approved setup.Students work on school-approved devices and accounts, following the school’s existing privacy and technology procedures.
  • 02
    Adults guide the activity.A teacher or facilitator introduces the steps, stays available for questions, and helps students work in pairs or individually.
  • 03
    Sharing stays classroom-friendly.Students can show a process, screenshot, or short explanation to the group. A public upload, chat, or leaderboard is not required.
